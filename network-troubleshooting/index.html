1. Technical Communication Skills Module 1

1.1 Communicating Technical Information Effectively

One of the most underrated skills in IT is the ability to explain complex technical concepts to people with no technical background. Your job isn't just to fix the problem — it's to make the user feel informed, respected, and confident that the issue is being handled.

1.2 Adapting to Your Audience

AudienceCommunication StyleExample
Non-technical userPlain language, analogies, no jargon, reassuring tone"Your computer is having trouble finding the internet — like trying to call someone with no signal. We'll fix it."
Technical userPrecise terminology, concise, collaborative"Looks like a DNS resolution failure — your DHCP lease is valid but the resolver isn't responding."
Manager/ExecutiveBusiness impact focused, brief, solution-oriented"The email server was down 45 minutes. Root cause was a misconfigured update. It's resolved and we've added monitoring to prevent recurrence."
Vendor/Peer technicianTechnical, collaborative, specific error codes and logs"Getting Event ID 7023, service fails to start with error 1053 — have you seen this with the latest patch?"

1.3 Active Listening Techniques

  • Let them finish: Never interrupt — the user may be building to the key detail
  • Take notes: Write down what they say — shows you're engaged and gives you a record
  • Clarifying questions: "When you say it's slow, can you describe what you're seeing?"
  • Paraphrase back: "So if I understand correctly, the issue started this morning after the Windows update, and only affects Outlook — is that right?"
  • Non-verbal cues: Nod, make eye contact, don't look at your phone or screen while they talk
  • Avoid assumptions: Don't decide the cause before you've heard everything

1.4 Communication Styles

StyleCharacteristicsIT Context
AssertiveDirect, confident, respectful — states needs clearly✅ Best — "I need you to restart the machine before I can proceed."
PassiveAvoids conflict, doesn't speak up, unclear⚠️ Problem — user doesn't know what's happening
AggressiveDemanding, dismissive, creates conflict❌ Never — "That's not how computers work, just do what I said."
Passive-AggressiveIndirect, sarcastic, undermining❌ Never — "Sure, I guess I'll fix it AGAIN."
The words you choose matter enormously. Say "I'll look into that for you" instead of "I don't know." Say "When would be a good time to follow up?" instead of going silent. Say "I understand this is frustrating" instead of "Calm down." The technical fix might take 5 minutes — rebuilding a user's trust after poor communication can take months.

2. Customer Service Fundamentals Module 1

2.1 Analyzing Customer Needs & Expectations

  • Stated needs: What the customer tells you they want — "Fix my email"
  • Underlying needs: What they actually need — "I need to be able to communicate with clients and not look incompetent"
  • Implicit expectations: Things they expect but don't say — fast response, regular updates, no condescension
  • Strategic questioning: Ask open-ended questions first ("Tell me what's happening"), then narrow down ("When did it start?")

2.2 The Customer Service Lifecycle in IT

  1. First contact: Greet professionally, introduce yourself, set the tone — first impression lasts
  2. Gather information: Listen, ask questions, take notes, repeat back to confirm understanding
  3. Set expectations: Provide a realistic timeframe — don't over-promise
  4. Resolve: Fix the issue — keep the user informed of progress, especially if it takes time
  5. Follow up: Confirm the fix worked from the user's perspective — not just yours
  6. Close the ticket: Document what happened and what was done

2.3 Delivering Bad News to a Customer

StepWhat to DoExample
1. AcknowledgeRecognize their frustration before diving into the technical explanation"I completely understand this has disrupted your work today."
2. Be directState the bad news clearly — don't bury it or be vague"Unfortunately, we won't be able to recover that file — it wasn't backed up."
3. Explain whyGive a clear, honest explanation — avoid technical jargon"The drive failed completely before we could make a copy."
4. Offer optionsPresent what CAN be done — don't just deliver the bad news and stop"We can try a data recovery service, though I can't guarantee results."
5. Prevent recurrenceExplain what will change going forward"We'll set up automatic backups today so this can't happen again."

2.4 IT Help Desk Fundamentals

  • Tier 1: First line support — password resets, basic troubleshooting, ticket creation, escalation
  • Tier 2: Technical specialists — deeper OS, network, application troubleshooting
  • Tier 3: Experts and vendors — engineering level, code changes, hardware replacement
  • SLA (Service Level Agreement): Defines response and resolution time expectations — e.g., Priority 1 = 1-hour response
  • Escalation path: Know when to escalate — don't spend 2 hours on something Tier 2 can solve in 10 minutes

3. The CLEAR Problem-Solving Framework Module 1

🔵 The CLEAR Framework
LetterStepWhat It Means in IT Support
CClarifyUnderstand the problem completely before attempting any fix. Ask questions, reproduce the issue, review error messages. "What exactly are you seeing? When did it start? What changed recently?"
LListenGive the customer your full attention. Don't assume you know the answer before they finish. Take notes. Reflect back what you heard to confirm accuracy.
EEmpathizeAcknowledge the impact of the problem. "I understand this is affecting your ability to work — let's get this resolved." People trust technicians who show they care.
AAnalyzeApply structured troubleshooting. Bottom-up OSI model. Test your theory. Eliminate variables one at a time. Don't change multiple things simultaneously.
RResolve & ReportFix the issue. Verify it works from the USER's perspective. Document everything — what the issue was, what you tried, what fixed it, and steps to prevent recurrence.

3.1 CompTIA Troubleshooting Methodology (Official Steps)

  1. Identify the problem — Question user, identify symptoms, review logs, duplicate if possible, back up data
  2. Establish theory of probable cause — Question the obvious, start simple (is it plugged in?), consider multiple approaches
  3. Test the theory to determine cause — Confirm or deny theory; if not confirmed, establish new theory or escalate
  4. Establish plan of action to resolve and identify potential effects — Know your fix and its side effects before touching anything
  5. Implement the solution or escalate as necessary — Apply fix; escalate if outside your expertise
  6. Verify full system functionality — Confirm fix worked AND that nothing else broke
  7. Document findings, actions, and outcomes — Write it down completely
The CompTIA methodology steps are the same across ALL CompTIA exams — A+, Network+, Security+, Server+. Memorize them once and know them cold. They will appear on every certification exam you take in this program. The order matters — especially step 4 (plan before you act) and step 7 (always document).

4. Conflict Resolution & Difficult Customers Module 1

4.1 Types of Difficult Customers

TypeBehaviorBest Approach
The Angry CustomerYelling, blaming, threateningStay calm, don't match their energy, acknowledge frustration, focus on solution — "I hear you. Let's fix this right now."
The Know-It-AllThinks they understand the problem better than youRespect their knowledge, ask them to walk you through what they've tried, involve them in the solution
The Impatient CustomerWants it fixed instantly, interrupts frequentlyGive frequent updates on progress — silence = frustration escalation. Set clear expectations on timing.
The Vague Customer"It just doesn't work" — can't describe the problemAsk specific yes/no questions: "Can you open a web browser?" "Does it show an error message?" Guide them step by step.
The Repeat CallerCalls for the same issue repeatedlyLook for root cause, not just symptom. Train the user. Consider a permanent fix vs. a band-aid.

4.2 De-escalation Techniques

  • Lower your own voice — When someone raises theirs, lowering yours signals calm and control
  • Use their name — "I understand, John. Let's work through this together."
  • Never say "calm down" — It never works and always makes things worse
  • Don't take it personally — They're frustrated at the situation, not at you as a person
  • Validate without agreeing — "I can see why that would be frustrating" — not "you're right, we messed up"
  • Focus on what you CAN do — Not what you can't. "What I can do right now is..."
  • Know when to escalate — If a customer becomes abusive, it's okay to say "I want to help you, but I need us to have a respectful conversation to do that."

4.3 Team Dynamics in IT Support

  • Know your team's skills: Who is the go-to person for networking? For Active Directory? Leverage expertise.
  • Communicate handoffs clearly: When transferring a ticket, give full context — don't make the customer repeat themselves
  • Own the issue: Even if you escalate, follow up to ensure it was resolved — the customer contacted YOU
  • Share knowledge: If you solve something novel, document it so the whole team benefits

6.2 Application Troubleshooting

  • Check Event Viewer first — Application log shows exact error code and faulting module
  • Run as Administrator: Many apps fail silently due to permission issues
  • Compatibility mode: Right-click → Properties → Compatibility — for older software
  • Reinstall with clean uninstall: Use Revo Uninstaller or similar to remove registry remnants before reinstalling
  • Check for conflicting software: Antivirus, VPNs, and security tools frequently interfere with other applications
  • SFC and DISM: Corrupted Windows files cause seemingly random application failures
  • .NET and Visual C++ Redistributables: Many apps silently require specific versions — install all of them

7. Network Diagnostics Module 2

7.1 Systematic Network Troubleshooting — OSI Bottom-Up

LayerCheckTool/Command
Layer 1 — PhysicalCable connected? Link light on? NIC enabled? Switch port active?Physical inspection, ipconfig (shows if NIC is up)
Layer 2 — Data LinkMAC address assigned? Switch seeing device? VLAN correct?arp -a, switch MAC table
Layer 3 — NetworkValid IP address? Correct subnet? Default gateway configured?ipconfig /all, ping [gateway]
Layer 4 — TransportPort open? Firewall blocking? Service listening?netstat -an, telnet host port
Layer 7 — ApplicationService running? Config correct? DNS resolving?nslookup, browser, application logs

7.2 Step-by-Step Connectivity Test

:: STEP 1 — Test local TCP/IP stack
ping 127.0.0.1
:: If this fails: NIC driver issue, reinstall network adapter

:: STEP 2 — Test own IP address
ipconfig /all               :: Note your IP — is it 169.254.x.x? (APIPA = DHCP failed)
ping [your own IP]
:: If this fails: possible IP conflict or NIC hardware issue

:: STEP 3 — Test default gateway
ping 192.168.1.1            :: Replace with your actual gateway
:: If this fails: cable/switch issue, wrong gateway configured

:: STEP 4 — Test internet by IP (bypasses DNS)
ping 8.8.8.8
:: If this fails but Step 3 works: ISP issue, WAN problem, firewall rule

:: STEP 5 — Test DNS resolution
ping google.com
nslookup google.com
:: If Step 4 works but Step 5 fails: DNS server issue
:: Fix: ipconfig /flushdns, change DNS to 8.8.8.8

:: STEP 6 — Trace the path
tracert google.com          :: Find exactly where packets stop

8. Practical Network Diagnostic Tools Module 2 & 4

8.1 Essential Network Diagnostic Commands

:: ===== PING — Test connectivity =====
ping google.com                 :: Basic ping (4 packets)
ping -t google.com              :: Continuous ping (Ctrl+C to stop)
ping -n 100 google.com          :: Send 100 packets
ping -l 1400 192.168.1.1        :: Large packet ping (test MTU)
ping -4 google.com              :: Force IPv4
ping -6 google.com              :: Force IPv6

:: ===== TRACERT — Trace path =====
tracert google.com              :: Show each hop to destination
tracert -d google.com           :: Don't resolve hostnames (faster)

:: ===== NSLOOKUP — DNS queries =====
nslookup google.com                     :: Default DNS lookup
nslookup google.com 8.8.8.8            :: Query specific DNS server
nslookup -type=MX tstc.edu             :: Query MX records
nslookup -type=NS tstc.edu             :: Query NS records

:: ===== NETSTAT — Connection info =====
netstat -an                     :: All connections (numeric)
netstat -b                      :: Show process for each connection (admin)
netstat -r                      :: Routing table
netstat -s                      :: Protocol statistics

:: ===== ARP — Layer 2 mapping =====
arp -a                          :: View ARP cache (IP to MAC)
arp -d *                        :: Clear ARP cache

:: ===== ROUTE — Routing table =====
route print                     :: Full routing table
route add 192.168.2.0 mask 255.255.255.0 192.168.1.1  :: Add route
route delete 192.168.2.0        :: Remove route

:: ===== PATHPING — Advanced ping + tracert =====
pathping google.com             :: Combines ping and tracert statistics

10.2 VPN Troubleshooting

:: Common VPN Issues and Fixes:

:: 1. Can't connect to VPN
::    - Check internet connectivity first (ping 8.8.8.8)
::    - Verify VPN server address is correct
::    - Check credentials
::    - Verify VPN client is up to date
::    - Check if firewall is blocking VPN ports (UDP 1194 for OpenVPN, UDP 500/4500 for IPsec)

:: 2. Connected to VPN but can't reach internal resources
::    - Check split vs full tunnel config
::    - Verify DNS — need internal DNS server when on VPN
::    - Check routing: route print
::    - Try pinging internal IP directly (not hostname)

:: 3. VPN drops frequently
::    - Check keep-alive settings
::    - Check internet connection stability
::    - Check for MTU mismatch: ping -l 1400 -f [vpn-server-ip]

:: 4. Slow performance on VPN
::    - Split tunnel to send only corporate traffic through VPN
::    - Check VPN server load
::    - Try different VPN protocol (IKEv2 is generally faster than L2TP)

12. Malware Prevention & Cyber Threats Module 4

12.1 Malware Removal Process

  1. Identify and verify — Confirm it's actually malware, not a false positive
  2. Quarantine — Disconnect from network immediately to prevent spread
  3. Disable System Restore — Prevents malware from hiding in restore points
  4. Boot into Safe Mode — Minimal environment makes removal easier
  5. Run multiple scanners — Malwarebytes + Windows Defender + secondary scanner
  6. Remove all detections — Follow scanner recommendations
  7. Re-enable System Restore, create restore point
  8. Patch everything — Close the vulnerability that allowed infection
  9. Change all passwords — Assume credentials were stolen
  10. Educate the user — How did this happen? Prevent recurrence

12.2 Prevention Best Practices

Keep everything patchedWindows Update, app updates, browser updates — most malware exploits known vulnerabilities
Antivirus/EDRWindows Defender is actually good now — keep it enabled and updated
User trainingPhishing awareness — the user is the weakest link and most common attack vector

DNS filteringBlock known malicious domains (Cisco Umbrella, Cloudflare Gateway)

13. Remote Security & Vulnerability Assessment Module 4

13.1 Vulnerability Scanning

  • Vulnerability scanner: Automated tool that checks systems for known security weaknesses (missing patches, weak configs, open ports)
  • Common tools: Nessus (industry standard), OpenVAS (free), Qualys, Rapid7 InsightVM
  • Authenticated vs unauthenticated scans: Authenticated scans log into the system and find more issues — always run both
  • CVE (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures): Standardized IDs for known vulnerabilities — e.g., CVE-2021-44228 (Log4Shell)
  • CVSS score: Common Vulnerability Scoring System — 0-10 severity score. 9+ = Critical, 7-8.9 = High

14. System Performance Tracking Module 4

14.1 Performance Baseline

A baseline is a snapshot of normal system performance taken when everything is working correctly. Without a baseline, you can't tell if current performance is abnormal. Capture baselines during normal business hours on a healthy system.

MetricNormal RangeConcern Threshold
CPU Utilization10–50% averageSustained >85% — investigate top consumers
Available RAM>25% free<10% — add RAM or reduce workload
Disk Queue Length<1>2 sustained — disk I/O bottleneck
Network Utilization<50% of link capacity>80% — bandwidth saturation
Disk Free Space>20% free<10% — clean up or expand storage
Page File UsageMinimalHigh page file use = insufficient RAM

14.2 Diagnosing Performance Issues

:: ===== CPU HIGH =====
:: Open Task Manager → CPU column → sort descending
:: Find culprit process, check if legitimate
:: Right-click → Open File Location to verify it's not malware
tasklist /fi "CPUtime gt 00:01:00"   :: Processes using >1 min CPU time

:: ===== MEMORY HIGH =====
:: Task Manager → Memory column → sort descending
:: Look for memory leaks (process growing continuously)
:: Check for VM balloon — virtual machine may be stealing RAM
wmic process get name,workingSetSize /format:list

:: ===== DISK 100% =====
:: Very common on systems with HDD (not SSD) running Windows 10/11

15. IT Support Documentation Module 5

15.1 Why Documentation Matters

  • Institutional memory: When you leave, your knowledge stays — documented in the system
  • Consistency: Every technician follows the same process — no tribal knowledge
  • Faster resolution: A documented solution to a recurring problem saves hours
  • Legal and compliance: Audit trails, change records, incident reports may be legally required
  • Training: New technicians can learn from documented solutions
  • Customer trust: Documented work shows professionalism — you can prove what you did and when

15.2 IT Support Documentation Types

Document TypePurposeKey Contents
Trouble TicketTrack a specific issue from open to closeUser, date/time, issue description, priority, actions taken, resolution, time spent
Knowledge Base ArticleStep-by-step solution to a recurring problemProblem description, symptoms, environment, solution steps, verification, related articles
Network DiagramVisual map of network infrastructurePhysical topology, IP addresses, device names, VLANs, WAN connections
Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)Step-by-step process for routine tasksPurpose, scope, materials needed, steps, verification, responsible party
Incident ReportFormal record of a significant outage or breachTimeline, impact, root cause, resolution, lessons learned, preventive actions

Asset InventoryTrack all hardware and software in the environmentDevice, model, serial number, location, assigned user, purchase date, warranty

16.2 Writing a Knowledge Base Article

📝 Good KB articles save 10x their writing time. Write one after every novel ticket you solve — future you will be grateful.

KNOWLEDGE BASE ARTICLE TEMPLATE:

TITLE: [Clear, searchable title that describes the problem]
Example: "Outlook fails to launch after Windows Update KB5034441"

ARTICLE ID: KB-2024-0042
Category: Email / Microsoft Office
Author: A. Medrano
Date Created: 2024-01-15
Last Updated: 2024-01-15
Applies To: Windows 10/11, Outlook 2019/365

SYMPTOMS:
- Outlook fails to open, error "Cannot start Microsoft Outlook"
- Error message references "outlook.exe - Application Error"
- Issue started after Windows Update installed on [date]
- Other Office apps (Word, Excel) work normally

ENVIRONMENT:
- Windows 10 22H2 / Windows 11 23H2
- Microsoft 365 Apps (Outlook version 16.x)
- Update KB5034441 installed

CAUSE:
Windows Update KB5034441 conflicts with a specific Outlook add-in
(identified as "Cisco Webex Meetings" add-in version 41.x).
The add-in causes Outlook to crash during initialization.

SOLUTION:
Step 1: Start Outlook in Safe Mode (disables add-ins)
    - Hold Ctrl while launching Outlook
    - Click Yes when prompted for Safe Mode

Step 2: Disable the conflicting add-in
    - File → Options → Add-ins
    - Manage: COM Add-ins → Go
    - Uncheck "Cisco Webex Meetings"
    - Click OK

Step 3: Restart Outlook normally — should launch successfully

Step 4: Update the Webex add-in to version 42.x or later
    - Download from cisco.com/go/webex
    - Run installer with admin rights

VERIFICATION:
Outlook launches normally without Safe Mode.
Webex meetings still accessible through updated add-in.

RELATED ARTICLES: KB-2024-0038 (Office activation issues after update)
TICKET REFERENCES: #2024-1187, #2024-1192, #2024-1205
